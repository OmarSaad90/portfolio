# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary: Prospective freelance clients — business owners across any industry, not scoped to a specific vertical — who have a website project in mind and are evaluating whether Omar is the right person to build it. Past clients span construction, law, education, and consulting, and those appear as real examples throughout the site, but copy should never read as if the work is limited to those industries. Secondary: recruiters and employers open to hiring a frontend/full-stack developer. The visitor arrives from their laptop or phone mid-search, likely comparing a few developers. They want to feel immediately confident the person understands real business needs, not only technical ones.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Omar, a web developer specializing in multi-page business websites. The site exists to convert curious visitors — primarily potential clients — into direct inquiries. Success means a visitor finishes reading and either sends a message or books a call, convinced that Omar genuinely cares about their specific project and has the execution quality to back it up.

## Brand Personality

Precise, warm, driven. The craft comes through in the work itself, not self-promotional copy. Tone is direct and human — talking to a person, not a hiring committee. Passion for building is visible and specific, never generic.

## Anti-references

- **Hacker-dark gradient**: dark backgrounds with purple/cyan/green glow, the "developer aesthetic" — signals tech for its own sake rather than business outcomes
- **Generic dev portfolio layout**: hero with typing animation, skills progress bars, identical icon-card grids, numbered section eyebrows (01 / 02 / 03)
- **Loud neon / synthwave**: hot pink, electric purple, neon cyan combinations
- **Agency-corporate**: stock photography, buzzword copy ("transform your digital presence"), faceless and interchangeable
- **Overly minimal**: white page, black text, nothing memorable — could be anyone

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't tell** — let the work and the process demonstrate skill; avoid writing "I'm passionate about" in copy when the work itself can say it
2. **Made for people, not peers** — the audience is a business owner, not another developer; every design choice should communicate ease and trust
3. **Specificity over claims** — real project details, real constraints, real outcomes beat generic "modern and responsive" bullets
4. **The portfolio is the proof** — this site is itself the first demonstration of what a client will receive; attention to detail here signals what their project will look like
5. **Direct and warm** — no corporate distance, no oversharing; the voice should feel like a skilled person who enjoys their work and will enjoy yours

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Full keyboard navigation. Sufficient color contrast (body text ≥ 4.5:1, large text ≥ 3:1). Respectful reduced-motion support for all animations — no content gated behind class-triggered transitions.
